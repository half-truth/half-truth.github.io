> It's because they mistakenly believe they're selling a product or service, when in fact they're selling money.  
> The wordy but correct motto for fintechs should be "Price and shape risk that others can't or won't".  
> The way to win this brutal game of "selling money" is not simply to take more risk, but to take different and smarter risk.  
>
> \- [Price and shape risk that others can't or won't](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/make-money-selling-money)  


> The technology shifts that enable new business models also require entirely new ways of measuring, delivering, and monetizing value. It's often insufficient to cobble together existing tools for that, hence the need for SBS.  
>
> \- [How to index an entire business model](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/how-to-index-an-entire-business-model)  

> Just like 'X for Y' patterns harm founders by focusing them on X rather than Y, the 'with AI' pattern can focus founders on the AI rather than the problem being solved.  
> Smart founders recognize that the goal isn't simply to build a company using AI – or any other specific technology or business model. The real goal is to build a large and successful company, and AI (or any other technology or business model) is just a means to an end.  
> 'With X' is a gambit to build momentum until you're better known for solving a problem really well than for how you solve it.  
>
> \- [Dangerous language in startups](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/dangerous-language)  

> While previous strategies made existing businesses more efficient, these emerging models do that, plus help create new businesses and grow existing ones.  
>
> \- [Invisible asymptotes in vertical software](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/invisible-asymptotes-vertical-software)  

> Risk, like energy, cannot be destroyed: only transferred or transformed.  
> The difference between success and failure is whether this transformation is intentional.  
> The friction you removed was managing something. Do you have data, technology, or expertise that makes you better at managing the new form?  
>
> \- [The thermodynamics of risk](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/thermodynamics-risk)  

> I think ghost markets appear most often when someone outside the market applies a label to the customer or to the solution which is entirely accurate, but also entirely useless.  
> The problem with ghost markets isn't that they're wrong; it's that there's no opportunity there.  
> It's critical to define the market you're going after in a way that's not only attractive to early hires and investors, but most importantly in a way that has a tangible answer to both WHO and WHAT.  
>
> \- [Vertical, horizontal, and ghost markets](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/ghost-markets)  

> There are only two ways I know to make money in business: bundling and unbundling.  
> The key to bundling or unbundling successfully is timing, timing, and timing.  
> The best founders are not only students of their customers and technology, but also of the market’s history and their place in it. Timing matters!  
>
> \- [Bundling, unbundling, and timing](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/bundling-unbundling-and-timing)  

> I think all great companies must have a great answer to 'why now?', and specifically to 'what makes NOW the uniquely right time to build THIS company?  
> The 'why now' puzzle piece has become so important to me because inertia is a crushing force. If there isn't a powerful reason for a given company to happen now, inertia will prevail.  
> Founders must find something that tilts the playing field towards them temporarily. These windows are hard to recognize in the moment, but if seen and exploited, they give startups a fleeting but critical edge to overcome the inertia of the status quo.  
>
> \- [Hedgehogs, foxes, and VCs](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/hedgehog-fox-vc)
