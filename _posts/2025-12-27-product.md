> For some things, time is more important than timing: long‑term investing, learning, and building relationships all reward you the longer you do them, because their benefits compound over time.  
> For other things, timing is more important than time: short‑term investing, starting a business, and advantageous genes all depend on acting at exactly the right moment; miss that window and you lose the opportunity.  
> What's the difference between these two classes of phenomena? Things that compound are all about time; the longer you do them, the faster the benefits build on each other. Things that are about exploiting a new opportunity are about timing; if you don't do them at the right time, you won't be rewarded. Try not to confuse the two.  
>
> \- [Time and Timing](https://borischerny.com/2022/09/07/time-and-timing.html)
