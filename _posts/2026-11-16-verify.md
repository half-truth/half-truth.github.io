> **Posterior ∝  Prior × exp(-Cumulative Loss)**
>
> \- [A General Framework for Updating Belief Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6430)

> So essentially: cybersecurity research is hard because of search difficulty: you have to look at a lot of things and do a lot of pruning to find issues, and models can make up for less pruning with more compute.  
> I think AI R&D requires much more 'research taste' than cybersecurity; finding new ways to improve LLM capabilities involves much more of having good intuitions about what will probably work and what won't.  
> The feedback loop for LLM experiments is much longer than for cybersecurity research because of asymmetry in how easily you can verify ideas.  
>
> \- [Why was cybersecurity automated before AI R&D?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xzPjd3ZF2oBK6ADFz/why-was-cybersecurity-automated-before-ai-r-and-d)

> And notice the jaggedness: the agents were superhuman at the verifiable part of the job (finding and chaining exploits) and poor at the hard-to-verify part (what was actually wanted of them).  
>
> \- [Making sense of Recursive Self Improvement](https://invertedpassion.com/making-sense-of-recursive-self-improvement/)

> It's not good enough to be first with an idea. You have to out-execute from day 1.  
> When your users stop asking for new features, this is a good indication it's time to pull in more people from the waitlist.  
>
> \- [Rethinking the startup MVP: Building a competitive product](https://linear.app/now/rethinking-the-startup-mvp-building-a-competitive-product)
